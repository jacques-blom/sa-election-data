# Coalition commentary: final eight-party audit

> Archived research before the copy review. Some claims and jokes below were corrected or removed. Use the [current copy catalogue](coalition-copy-inventory.md) and [fact-check decisions](coalition-copy-audit.json) for the implemented version.

Researched 7 November 2026. This audits the 28 pairings among ANC, DA, EFF, MK, IFP, PA, FF+ and ActionSA. It is not a claim to cover every South African party or every political episode.

The implemented catalogue has 89 relationship and local-history profiles (65 local, 22 national, 2 provincial), plus eight national solo fallbacks. There are 207 fictional scenario definitions. The first-pick bank separately supplies **96 short comments across 24 sourced backstories**, twelve lines and three distinct hooks per featured party.

## Research and editorial limits

- DA–EFF and DA–MK refusals are attributed to the DA’s July 2024 statement and Helen Zille’s November 2025 interview. Earlier issue-by-issue EFF support for DA-led councils remains a separate, accessible chapter. The original catalogue omitted the national red lines; local cooperation stories alone did not cover them.
- The EFF’s June 2024 exclusion of DA and FF+ is a dated national position. The FF+’s May 2026 policy disagreement with MK is not paraphrased as an absolute ban.
- ActionSA’s openness to MK talks, reported on 6 November 2026, is not described as an agreement. Criticism and accusations from parties are not adopted as fact.
- The IFP–EFF breakup uses both parties’ January 2023 accounts. The dispute concerned particular municipal arrangements, especially uMhlathuze; it does not prove that every council had the same arrangement.
- The January 2023 ActionSA offer of two Joburg mayoral committee posts is explicitly an offer reported by ActionSA, not a completed allocation. Johannesburg’s annual report confirms EFF and PA in the wider January 2023 unity coalition, rather than a bilateral pact.
- June 2024 cabinet appointments are historical hooks. Steenhuisen and Groenewald are described in those dated roles; the text does not override the separate current sprite roster.
- KZN’s largest-party versus premier story is provincial history. The actual 41–39 vote is not substituted for the game’s municipal majority rule.
- Generic DA spreadsheet and calculator jokes were removed: they were invented office humour, not a recognisable political reference. Budget and tie-break stories now name the actual historical trigger.
- Sources include official government records, party statements, direct reporting, and ACCORD research. Web search was supplemented with targeted Firecrawl reads. Large-PDF fetch failures were checked against indexed passages and corroborating council material; no failed fetch was treated as evidence.

## Display and randomness

The bird’s copy was rewritten as a roast: all 96 first-pick lines and all 89 relationship/local-history lines now lead with a short punchline. The first-pick bank keeps twelve lines per featured party, with no dates in the visible jokes. Dates, explanations and source links stay in the backstory. The targets are political contradictions, defections, bargaining and public personas; the reference should be recognisable to a politically interested South African without reading an explanatory setup. Supporting Charter-to-GNU and Charter-to-Tshwane context is included in the affected backstories; the DA’s “rescue” campaign wording also links to its actual 2024 manifesto.

Changing the selection draws a comment automatically; the bird is not a hidden tap control. A first pick uses the party’s introduction bank or its applicable local solo history. Newly completed documented public-position pairings are prioritised, then other newly completed relationships. Other changes mix relevant backstories with accurate seat-based commentary. Unseen lines are preferred over repeats.

No timed carousel is used. An unchanged live refresh, even with a new source timestamp, preserves the comment and its source disclosure. Actual changed seat figures trigger a fresh relevant draw. Removing a party removes its pair stories. Historical comments’ source disclosures follow the exact joke selected; general seat commentary can still expose a relevant backstory from the deal.

Satire is the bird’s authored commentary, not a quotation attributed to a leader. Each relationship still has both a fictional rupture and a truce. Actors use the same rules and probabilities, without party-specific stability scores or arithmetic penalties.
