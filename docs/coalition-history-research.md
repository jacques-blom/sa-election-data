# Coalition Chaos: local-history research ledger

> Archived research before the copy review. Some claims and jokes below were corrected or removed. Use the [current copy catalogue](coalition-copy-inventory.md) and [fact-check decisions](coalition-copy-audit.json) for the implemented version.

Researched 7 November 2026. Implemented in [local-history.ts](../src/lib/seats/local-history.ts) and [political-history.ts](../src/lib/seats/political-history.ts).

## Coverage and interpretation

The full catalogue now has **65 local profiles across 36 municipalities**, including **all eight metros and all nine provinces**. A profile is a selected party relationship or single-party local backstory; multiple profiles may draw on the same historical episode. There are 64 distinct source URLs behind these local profiles. The latest additions and the full eight-party pairing audit are recorded in [coalition-commentary-research.md](coalition-commentary-research.md).

The selection prioritises metros, repeatedly contested councils and regional centres. It is not a ranking of site traffic or a claim to cover every municipality. Sources span municipal records, party statements, MISTRA and ACCORD research, and contemporary reporting. Historical episodes are dated; they do not establish who governs in November 2026.

Each entry supplies one short joke, an expandable factual backstory with links, and two explicitly fictional outcomes: a rupture and a truce. Party arithmetic is identical. Either selected partner can leave; a single-party story withholds half its delegation. Historical votes, electoral arrangements and internal leadership changes are distinguished from formal governing coalitions.

National pairings and the eight major-party fallback histories remain available where no matching local history exists. A council's local story requires its IEC municipality ID and every named party in the player's selection. Unknown combinations still get the general scenario deck. Real organisation-name aliases enable small-party histories even when the source supplies a full uppercase name rather than an acronym.

## Editorial decisions

- **Witzenberg:** timeline sources disagreed about the exact partner lineup. The game uses the municipality-confirmed December 2023 mayoral appointment and reported DA return, without guessing the coalition membership.
- **Tshwane, Mangaung and eThekwini:** backing a candidate or confidence vote is described as voting support, not automatically a formal coalition.
- **Buffalo City and Msunduzi:** the source supports an internal ANC leadership change. These are caucus stories, not invented coalition collapses.
- **Cape Town:** the 2006 coalition is explicitly a historical prequel; it does not imply the city currently requires a coalition.
- **Modimolle-Mookgophong:** the actual 2021 coin toss is a backstory. The game's ordinary majority target still applies.
- **Party statements:** factual announcements and the party's stated explanation are separated. Accusations are not adopted as an agreed account of blame.
- **Available local data:** some researched parties are absent from the current saved seat data, so those pair stories correctly remain dormant. No party or seats are inserted to make a joke appear.

## Council-by-council sources

### Eastern Cape

#### Buffalo City · metro

IEC municipality ID: 1117.

- **ANC — The change comes from inside** (Buffalo City · March 2023). The ANC recalled Buffalo City mayor Xola Pakati in March 2023. Princess Faku replaced him on 23 March, winning 62 votes to the DA candidate’s 22. The ANC held a majority; this was an internal leadership change, not a coalition collapse. [SABC · 23 Mar 2023](https://www.sabcnews.com/sabcnews/princess-faku-elected-as-buffalo-city-metro-mayor/).

#### Dr. Beyers Naude

IEC municipality ID: 1102.

- **DA + CSA — The Graaff-Reinet last-minute deal** (Dr Beyers Naudé · 23 November 2021). The DA announced a coalition with Compatriots of South Africa and FF+ shortly before Dr Beyers Naudé’s inaugural November 2021 council meeting. DA’s Ewald Loock became mayor and CSA’s Laughton Hoffman became speaker. [DA statement · 23 Nov 2021](https://www.da.org.za/2021/11/da-cllr-ewald-loock-elected-as-mayor-of-the-dr-beyers-naude-municipality).

- **DA + FF+ — The Karoo’s just-in-time pact** (Dr Beyers Naudé · November 2021). DA, FF+ and Compatriots of South Africa concluded a coalition agreement shortly before Dr Beyers Naudé’s inaugural council meeting on 23 November 2021. The DA’s Ewald Loock was elected mayor. [DA statement · 23 Nov 2021](https://www.da.org.za/2021/11/da-cllr-ewald-loock-elected-as-mayor-of-the-dr-beyers-naude-municipality).

#### Nelson Mandela Bay · metro

IEC municipality ID: 1001.

- **ANC + EFF — The Bay’s musical chairs** (Nelson Mandela Bay · May 2023). In May 2023, a coalition including ANC and EFF helped replace DA mayor Retief Odendaal with Gary van Niekerk of the Northern Alliance. Van Niekerk had been council speaker. This is a historical leadership change, not today’s coalition map. [eNCA · 26 May 2023](https://www.enca.com/news/northern-alliances-gary-van-niekerk-elected-new-nelson-mandela-bay-mayor); [ACCORD · Bay coalition timeline](https://coalitions.accord.org.za/coalition/nelson-mandela-bay/).

- **DA + UDM — The Bay’s deputy-mayor breakup** (Nelson Mandela Bay · 2016–2017). DA and UDM were partners in Nelson Mandela Bay’s post-2016 coalition, alongside ACDP and COPE. In August 2017, a no-confidence vote removed UDM deputy mayor Mongameli Bobani, deepening the dispute between the partners. [Nelson Mandela Bay annual report · 2016/17](https://www.nelsonmandelabay.gov.za/DataRepository/Documents/adopted-2016-17-annual-report.pdf); [DA statement · 24 Aug 2017](https://www.da.org.za/2017/08/da-welcomes-successful-motion-no-confidence-bobani).
