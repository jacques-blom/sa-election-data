## Native search in the main final-answer request

**A DA–ActionSA or DA–ANC partnership could each form a majority in Johannesburg’s 2026 council**, on the current estimates. Both are politically possible but face leadership obstacles; neither is a confirmed agreement.

The council has **270 seats; 136 are needed for a majority**. The largest estimated allocations are DA 96, ANC 55, ActionSA 44, EFF 18 and MK 15. **These are provisional estimates, not final seat allocations, and updates are delayed.**

- **DA + ActionSA: 140 seats**, four above a majority. DA mayoral candidate Helen Zille has favoured this route, but ActionSA leader Herman Mashaba has said he would work with the DA only if Zille were not involved. ([timeslive.co.za](https://www.timeslive.co.za/politics/elections-2026/2026-11-06-zille-favours-actionsa-or-anc-coalition-in-joburg/?utm_source=openai))
- **DA + ANC: 151 seats**, 15 above. Their national partnership provides a precedent, but Johannesburg ANC leaders have expressed reservations about a deal involving Zille. ([timeslive.co.za](https://www.timeslive.co.za/politics/elections-2026/2026-11-06-zille-favours-actionsa-or-anc-coalition-in-joburg/?utm_source=openai))
- **A route without the DA: 136 seats**, exactly a majority, if the parties recorded as members of the previous administration reunite and add the ATM. That is an illustration, not a reported agreement; current willingness by all those parties, including the ATM, is unverified.

The decisive question is **who would be mayor and what concessions parties will accept**. The scenarios show potential voting strength, not who has agreed to govern together.
